Arcforge

With Arcforge 2.5.0 or later installed, a Small Wireless Bridge puts an Arcforge machine on a network to read, control, power and feed it; Pressurized drives store Arcforge's gases; and the Arc Crusher grinds the mod's ores into dusts.

Arcforge is an optional companion mod with machines, gases and a crusher. With it installed, Encoded Logistics adds three things:

  1. Machines on the network through the Small Wireless Bridge: read them, switch them, set them up, power them and feed them from the network, all from the Terminal OS.
  2. Gas storage: Arcforge's gases go into Pressurized Storage Drives.
  3. Dusts: the Arc Crusher grinds the mod's ores, raw metals and ingots into dusts.

Without Arcforge none of this is there, and nothing else in the mod changes.

Which Arcforge works#

Encoded Logistics talks to Arcforge only through Arcforge's published API, which came in Arcforge 2.5.0. At startup it reads the API's version from the Arcforge that is installed and turns on only what that version supports:

Arcforge APIMachines (Small Wireless Bridge)Gas storage
None (Arcforge before 2.5.0)OffOff
1.0OnOff
1.1 or a later 1.xOnOn
2.0 or laterOffOff

Whatever it turns off, the server log says why: look for "Arcforge integration disabled" or "Arcforge gas storage disabled", or "Arcforge integration enabled" when all is well. With the integration off, Small Wireless Bridges already placed are kept but do nothing, and every machine command answers ELC1318. Nothing is lost: installing a matching Arcforge brings them back as they were.

Machines through the Small Wireless Bridge#

Putting one on#

Use the bridge on a face of any block that is an Arcforge machine: a single-block machine, any block of a formed multiblock, or an unformed multiblock's controller. It goes on as a small 10 x 10 module in front of that face, so the space there has to be free.

  • One per machine: a whole multiblock takes one.
  • Only the machine's owner (when Arcforge records one) or a server operator may put one on.
  • To take it off, mine it (with a pickaxe), or sneak-use the machine's face with an empty hand (this needs the Firewall's BUILD permission while it is linked). Breaking the machine drops it.

Linking it to a network#

It joins a network over the air, as a Wireless Bridge or Wireless Port does. Take the network's Wireless Controller with a Link Card, then use the card on the bridge (or the machine). That needs the Firewall's BUILD permission on the network. Linked, it uses one of the Access Points' client slots, is a one-lane device on the network, and drains rack.smallWirelessBridgeDrain (default 1) FE/t over the air. Unlinking it at the controller (its Linked tab) takes it off the network.

LEDMeans
Yellow, blinkingNot linked, or not connected
Light blueOnline
AmberA fault: no Access Points, no free client slot, or the machine missing, unformed or faulted

Looking at the bridge shows its own popup (its controller, the machine, its lane and drain, its Gateway and power from network); looking at the machine shows the machine's status, progress and time left, energy, heat and what it is making. The popups need the Firewall's VIEW permission.

Device names#

A bridged machine is a device named after its type: the first three letters of each of its first two words, or the first six letters of a one-word type, then a number. An Arc Crusher (arcforge:arc_crusher) is ARCCRU01; a type of one word such as arcforge:electrolyzer is ELECTR01; a type with no name is MCH01. Work with Devices lists it as a Machine.

What the network can do with a machine#

  • Read it: its status and the reason for it, the current operation and its outputs, energy, heat, tanks, statistics and settings.
  • Switch it on or off, and change its redstone mode (ignore, high, low, pulse, throttle), each side's mode (top, bottom, left, right, back, front) and auto-eject. Every change goes through Arcforge's own rules: a setting the machine doesn't have is ELC1320, one it refuses is ELC1321, and an unformed multiblock is ELC1319.
  • Power it from the network (below).
  • Feed it from a Gateway (below) so crafting jobs can use it.
  • React to it: the trigger events *MCHIDLE, *MCHFAULT and *MCHNOPWR fire as the machine goes idle, faults or runs short of power, and *MCHDONE for each operation it finishes, with &DATA such as ARCCRU01 5 minecraft:bone_meal.
  • Graph it: a Monitoring Server samples each machine's operations a minute and FE stored once a second, for Display Panel graphs (*MCHOPS, *MCHFE). That history isn't saved.

Reading (Work with Machines, the RTVMCH* commands, the popups) needs the Firewall's VIEW permission; changing a machine needs BUILD.

WRKMCH lists the network's bridged machines: name, type, status (bright when it needs attention), progress, energy and operations a minute.

Power from the network#

CHGMCHCFG PWRNET(*YES) (or 2=Change) tops the machine's FE up from the network each tick:

  • up to rack.machinePowerRate (default 1000) FE/t,
  • at rack.machinePowerEfficiency (default 0.9): with the default, 10 FE leave the network for every 9 that arrive,
  • and never taking the network's stored energy below rack.machinePowerReserve (default 0.25) of its capacity, so the network keeps running.

Energy the machine has no room for stays in the network.

Feeding it from a Gateway#

CHGMCHCFG GATEWAY(GATEWAY01) (or 2=Change) makes the machine one of that Gateway's machines, over the air, as if it stood beside it. The Gateway puts a Processing Schematic's inputs in through the machine's own slot and tank rules (inputs, fuel and catalysts only) and takes the outputs its runs are waiting for out of the machine (items, fluids and gases), so the crafting job they belong to completes. Machines beside the Gateway are fed first. The Gateway and the machine must be on the same network.

Gases#

With Arcforge's API 1.1 or later, Arcforge's gases (its fluids that are lighter than air, or tagged #arcforge:gases) are stored as pressurized resources: they go into Pressurized Storage Drives, in mB, and show on the terminals' Pressurized tab. They move through the same ports and parts as fluids, and Arcforge's Gas Cartridges fill and empty at the terminals. Without Arcforge there is nothing for a Pressurized drive to hold.

Arc Crusher dusts#

With Arcforge installed, the Arc Crusher grinds the mod's three metals. These nine recipes load only when Arcforge is there; each takes 200 ticks:

FromGivesBonus
Neodymium, Tantalum or Gallium ore (#c:ores/...)2 dust1 more at a 25% chance
Raw Neodymium, Tantalum or Gallium (#c:raw_materials/...)1 dust1 more at a 25% chance
A Neodymium, Tantalum or Gallium Ingot (#c:ingots/...)1 dustNone

The dusts themselves are on Ores and metals.
