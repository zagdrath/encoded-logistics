Segments

Splitting a cable run into separate networks with the Segment Isolator, and using those segments from racks, Trunk Ports and routers.

On this page
  1. Segment Isolator
  2. Using a segment elsewhere
  3. Segments stay apart
  4. Segment IDs

A Segment Isolator cuts a cable run in two. The two sides become separate networks, called segments: lanes, storage, devices and energy don't cross it. Segments let you keep parts of a base apart (a public storage network beside a private one, say) while still letting racks and routers reach across in controlled ways.

Segment Isolator#

  • Placing. It's an inline housing, placed like a log: its axis follows the face you click. It connects only on its two ends, so put it in line with the cable you want to cut.
  • What it does. The network on each side stops at the isolator. Each side is its own network, with its own lanes, storage, devices and energy.
  • Cost. It uses no lanes. It drains power.isolatorDrain (default 0.5) FE per tick, and since it's on both networks, each side pays that.
  • Its light. The amber light is on while both ends are attached to network blocks: it's actually separating two segments. Jade shows "Isolating two segments" or "Idle".

Each side needs its own controller

The side without a controller isn't a network on its own: its devices are offline. That's also what makes isolators useful for separating two controllers: two controllers on one network are a conflict, but with an isolator between them each runs its own segment.

Using a segment elsewhere#

A segment is named by a Link Card. Sneak-use the card on the isolator to store the segment on the half you clicked ("Segment on this side stored"). You can then use the card on:

Use the card onWhat it doesGuide
A switch's unit in an open Server RackThe rack learns the segment, and its pooled devices can be set to serve it. A rack knows up to five segments besides its own network.Switches and VLANs
A Trunk PortThe Trunk Port carries the segment into the network it's on, for every rack on that network.Trunk Port, Switches and VLANs
A Router's unitThe Router links to that segment's network, to route between them.Routers
An Optical MultiplexerOne of its channels carries the segment over a fibre line.Optical networking

Using the card again on the same rack switch or Trunk Port unlinks the segment. Storing or using a segment needs the Firewall's BUILD permission on that network, if it has one.

Segments stay apart#

Segments never see each other's storage, even when a rack or a Trunk Port serves several of them. Only two things move items between segments:

  • an L3 Switch, with routes between the segments its rack knows;
  • a Router, with routes between the networks it's linked to.

Both are rack devices; see Switches and VLANs and Routers.

Segment IDs#

Racks and Trunk Ports give the segments they know a number, a VLAN-style segment ID, used in their panels, routes and port settings:

  • In a Server Rack, its own network is segment 1, and each segment you link gets the next free ID (up to 4094).
  • On a Trunk Port, linked segments get IDs 10, 20, 30 and so on, up to eight segments.

VLAN trunking itself (access and trunk ports, native segments, what each rack device serves) is explained on Switches and VLANs.
