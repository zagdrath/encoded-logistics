Networks

What a network is, which blocks are devices and which just carry the network on, and how to tell whether it's online.

On this page
  1. What a network is
  2. Devices and pass-through blocks
  3. The pages in this section
  4. Seeing what's going on

Everything in Encoded Logistics happens on a network: storage, terminals, ports, autocrafting, racks and scripts. This section explains how networks are built and how they share out their two resources, lanes and energy.

What a network is#

A network is everything joined to one controller structure: a group of Network Controller blocks, a Server Rack holding rack Network Controllers, or a Midrange System. From the controller, the network reaches every block that connects toward its neighbour, through cables and through devices, and on through remote links such as a paired Network Bridge or a lanes Point-to-Point Link. Two blocks are linked only when both connect on the touching faces: a cable side and the neighbour's face.

A network has one controller structure. Blocks with no controller structure on their network are simply offline: a Drive Bay or a terminal cabled to nothing but other devices does nothing until a controller joins them. Two controller structures that end up on one network shut it down until one goes; see conflicts.

The network is worked out again whenever something on it is placed, broken or reconnected, from the next tick. A controller structure that's partly in unloaded chunks waits, with its devices offline, until all of it is loaded again.

Devices and pass-through blocks#

The blocks on a network come in three kinds:

KindWhat it doesExamples
DeviceNeeds lanes to work; switched online and offline every tick.Drive Bay, terminals and other parts, Fabricator, Relay Antenna, a Server Rack
Pass-throughUses no lanes; carries the network on through it.Cables, Power Inlet, Capacitor Bank, Network Bridge
End pointOn the network, but the network stops there.Segment Isolator, Access Point

Most devices pass the network on as well: a Drive Bay with cables on two sides joins them, just as a cable would. A cable with parts mounted on it is both: still a cable carrying its lanes, and a device needing a lane for each part.

A device is online when the network is running (valid, powered and not in conflict) and the device got its lanes. Offline devices keep their contents; a Drive Bay's drives just can't be reached until it's back.

  1. Network Controllers: the Network Controller's shapes, energy and lanes, its screen, the rack and Midrange controllers, and conflicts.
  2. Cables: the three cable tiers, their 13 colours, and the Cable Anchor and Cable Facade.
  3. Lanes: how lanes are handed out, nearest first, and what to do when they run out.

Terminals are parts too, but they have their own section: Terminals and access. Joining networks over distance (bridges, fibre, wireless, routing between them) is in Networking.

Seeing what's going on#

  • The Network screen. Right-click any block of a Network Controller structure: status, energy, lanes and every device on the network. See the controller screen.
  • Jade. With Jade installed, looking at a cable shows Lanes used / carried (red when it's full), and a controller shows its status, lanes and structure.
  • The Terminal OS. WRKDEV lists every device on the network with its lanes and status, and DSPNETSTS shows the network's status at a glance. See Terminal OS and ELCL.
