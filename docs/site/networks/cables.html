Cables

Network Cable, Dense Network Cable and Fiber Cable in 13 colours, how they connect and recolour, and the Cable Anchor and Cable Facade.

On this page

Cables join network blocks together and carry lanes between them. There are three tiers, each in a neutral colour and twelve dyes, and two attachments that change how a cable joins or looks: the Cable Anchor and the Cable Facade.

The three tiers#

TierLanesDrain per blockLook
Network Cablelanes.perCable (default 8)lanes.cableDrain (default 0.05) FE/tSlim
Dense Network Cablelanes.perDenseCable (default 32)lanes.denseCableDrain (default 0.2) FE/tThick
Fiber Cablelanes.perFiberCable (default 32)lanes.fiberCableDrain (default 0.1) FE/tSlim

A cable uses no lanes itself; it only carries them. Its drain is paid while its network runs.

  • Network Cable is the everyday cable. Eight lanes is enough for a few devices.
  • Dense Network Cable carries four times as many lanes. Use it for trunks: the runs from the controller that everything else hangs off.
  • Fiber Cable carries as many lanes as Dense in the slim profile, and it's the only cable that carries optical lines (Optical Multiplexer channels). Very long continuous fibre runs lose lanes to span loss, which Optical Amplifiers fix. Looking at a Fiber Cable shows a popup with its network, its span length, and its lanes in use out of those left after loss. See Optical networking.

All three tiers join each other. A link between two blocks carries the smaller of their two capacities, so a Network Cable spliced into a Dense run limits that run to the Network Cable's lanes. See Lanes.

Connections#

A cable connects on each side to:

  • another cable of a compatible colour (see below), of any tier;
  • a network block that connects on that face: a controller, a Power Inlet (not its port), a Drive Bay (not its front), a device;
  • nothing, otherwise.

Cables connect by themselves when you place them. A side with a part or a Cable Anchor on it never connects.

Colours#

Every tier comes in neutral and twelve dyes. The rule is simple:

  • Neutral joins every colour.
  • A dyed cable joins only its own colour and neutral.

So two parallel runs in different colours never merge, and a neutral cable can tap into either. Black, brown, grey and light grey cables don't exist. A cable's tooltip says which colours it joins.

Dyeing cables#

In the world. Use a dye on a placed cable to recolour it; it uses up one dye. Anything mounted on the cable (parts, anchors, facades) stays where it is. Use a water bucket on a dyed cable to wash it back to neutral (the bucket stays full). A water bucket on a neutral cable waterlogs it, as on any waterloggable block.

Cable Anchor#

The Cable Anchor stops one side of a cable connecting, for this cable and its neighbour alike. Use it to keep two parallel cables of the same colour from joining, or to stop a cable linking into a block next to it.

Use it on a cable: it goes on the face you click. Since a connected side is covered by the cable's arm, the easiest way is to anchor the side first and then place the neighbouring cable or block. To take an anchor off, use a wrench on it, or sneak-use it with an empty hand.

Cable Facade#

A Cable Facade is a panel that covers one side of a cable, and it can be dressed to look like a block, so cables can run hidden in walls and floors.

  • Mounting. Use a facade on a cable. It goes on the side your crosshair points toward from the cable's centre (connected sides included); if that side is taken, it goes on the face you clicked. While you hold a facade and look at a cable, a ghost preview shows where it will go. A facade doesn't stop the side connecting.
  • Dressing. Use a block on a mounted facade to dress it in that block. It uses up one of the block and gives back any block the facade had before. The block must be a full, solid, non-translucent cube without a block entity, and not on the facades.blocklist list (empty by default). An opaque facade hides the neighbouring block's face, just as a full block would.
  • Removing. Sneak-use with an empty hand: the first time takes the dressing block off, the second takes the facade off. A wrench takes it off too. Broken, it drops a blank facade and its block.

Mining and pick-block#

Mining a cable takes off the facade or part you're looking at first, and the cable itself only once nothing is in the way. Pick-block gives the part or facade you're looking at, or the cable.

Parts on cables#

Terminals, ports, taps, sensors, planes and links mount on the faces of cables. A cable carrying parts is still a cable (it carries its tier's lanes) and is also a device needing a lane for each part. Parts can also be mounted straight on the face of a network block; the mod then places an internal block, the Part Host (encodedlogistics:part_host, which has no item), to hold the part. See Cable parts.

A cable carrying a Threshold Sensor (or a redstone Point-to-Point Link output) sends out redstone, and only then does redstone dust connect to it.

Firewall#

On a network with a Firewall, placing or breaking network blocks, and putting parts, anchors or facades on its cables, needs the BUILD permission. See Firewall and security.
