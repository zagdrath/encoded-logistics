The basics#

  • Height: devices are 1 to 10U. A device needs that many free units in a row.
  • Lanes: a device uses one lane of the network unless noted below. An L2 or L3 Switch in the same rack can pool lanes for the others, so they cost the network nothing. The rack Network Controllers use none.
  • Power: each device drains its own FE/t from the network's energy while the network runs. The figures below are config options in the rack section (optical for the optical gear and chassis); see the config reference.
  • Panel: click a device in the rack screen for its settings panel. Changing settings needs Rack access, and some need Build (noted below).
  • Popup: look at a device through the open front door for its status and figures.

Security and power#

Firewall#

Firewall (1U) decides who may use its network: six permissions per player, a default policy, and an owner who always has full access. One per network; a second one shows a fault ("another Firewall is in use") and is ignored. Drains rack.firewallDrain (default 2) FE/t. Its panel lists the players and their permissions. It's described in full on Firewall and security.

UPS#

UPS (2U) is a battery for its network. When the network's supply falls short of what it drains, the UPS covers the difference from its own battery in the same tick, before the network's own buffers are touched. While supply is good it recharges from the top half of the network's buffers.

  • Modes (a button in its panel): Online always conditions the supply, covering any shortfall and recharging all the time; Standby only steps in once the supply has failed altogether, and draws less. Online drains rack.upsDrainOnline (default 2) FE/t, Standby rack.upsDrainStandby (default 1).
  • Capacity and rates: rack.upsCapacity (default 1,000,000) FE, giving up to rack.upsMaxOutput (default 8192) FE/t and recharging at up to rack.upsMaxInput (default 8192) FE/t.
  • Several UPSes on a network add up: they discharge together and recharge in parallel.
  • Overload: a shortfall beyond what the UPSes can give is a fault.
  • On battery: its status LED turns amber (blinking below rack.upsLowBatteryPercent (default 20) % charge, "Low battery"), its LCD scrolls "ON BATTERY" with the runtime left, and it sounds an alarm: four beeps every 30 seconds, a rapid beep while the battery is low, and one beep when power comes back.
  • Muting: the panel's mute button silences the alarm for the current outage only. The server option rack.upsAlarms (default true) turns UPS alarms off for everyone; the client option audio.upsAlarmSounds (default true) silences them for you alone.
  • Panel: battery gauge, charge, load, runtime and source, the mode, a load bar, and the last three switchovers.
  • Priority: High by default, so it keeps its lane when the rack runs short.

2U and 4U Network Controllers#

2U Network Controller (2U) and 4U Network Controller (4U) are the network's core in rack form: a rack holding one is a lane source and the network's energy buffer, as a Network Controller structure is.

  • A 2U counts as rack.rackController2uEnergyBlocks (default 20) controller blocks for energy, receive rate and drain, and gives rack.rackController2uLaneFaces (default 6) times lanes.perControllerFace lanes. A 4U is twice that.
  • Redundant pair: two of the same size on one network are active and standby. The standby keeps its own buffer topped up, draws rack.rackControllerStandbyFactor (default 0.1) of its drain, and takes over within a second if the active one loses power, faults or is taken out. The panel's "Switch over" button does it on purpose (it asks you to confirm).
  • Panel: energy, status, usage, devices, a lanes bar, the pair's roles and last failover, and a chip for each of the rack's connection points (up, down, unused or mismatch).
  • They use no lanes and have High priority. Their device names are NETWORK_CONTROLLER_2U01 and NETWORK_CONTROLLER_4U01.

Their rules (sizes that can pair, conflicts with other controllers) are on Network Controllers.

Networking#

24- and 48-Port L2 Switches#

24-Port L2 Switch and 48-Port L2 Switch (1U each) give the other devices in their rack lanes of their own: a pool of rack.l2Switch24Lanes (default 16) or rack.l2Switch48Lanes (default 32) lanes, so the network only carries the switch's one-lane uplink. Pooled devices can also be put on other segments, as access ports or VLAN trunks. Drains rack.switchDrain (default 2) FE/t, High priority by default. The panel lists the rack's devices, which are pooled and which segment each serves, and has a Ports view. See Switches and VLANs.

L3 Switch#

L3 Switch (1U) is an L2 Switch with a rack.l3SwitchLanes (default 32)-lane pool that also moves items between the segments its rack knows: up to 8 routes and 8 QoS rules, at rack.l3SwitchRate (default 32) items a second. Drains rack.l3SwitchDrain (default 3) FE/t. Panel tabs: Devices, Routes, QoS. See Switches and VLANs.

Router#

Router (1U) moves items between separate networks, however far apart and in whichever dimension, linked to it with a Link Card. Up to 6 networks and 6 routes, each Move or Share, with filters; static or dynamic routing; ACLs. Three Optical Transceiver cages raise its rate. Drains rack.routerDrain (default 4) FE/t plus rack.routerDrainPerTransceiver (default 1) per transceiver. Panel tabs: Routes, Mode, Table, ACLs. See Routers.

Wireless Controller#

Wireless Controller (1U) runs its network's wireless: it links Handheld Terminals so they work anywhere, and it links Wireless Bridges, Wireless Ports and Small Wireless Bridges as its clients, served by the network's Access Points. Drains rack.wirelessControllerDrain (default 4) FE/t. It faults ("No access points") when it has clients and no Access Point is online. Panel tabs: APs and Linked. See Wireless.

Directory Server (Rack)#

Directory Server (Rack) (1U) does the Directory Server block's job in a rack: it publishes chosen devices of its network so other networks' Terminal OS can reach them as SYSNAME/DEVICE, and logs lookups. Drains rack.directoryDrain (default 2) FE/t. Panel tabs: Published, Remote, Lookups.

Optical Multiplexer (Rack)#

Optical Multiplexer (Rack) (1U) does the Optical Multiplexer block's job in a rack: up to optical.multiplexerChannels (default 8) client networks over one fibre line to its partner. Its line is a Fiber Cable leaving the rack at any connection point. Faults when it's paired but the line is down. Drains optical.multiplexerDrain (default 4) FE/t. Panel tabs: Channels, Line. Optical Amplifier (Rack)#

Optical Amplifier (Rack) (1U) regenerates the optical lines that pass through its rack: a multiplexer's or shelf's line can come in at one connection point and leave at another, and its span loss starts again from zero. It needs Fiber Cable at two or more of the rack's points; with fewer it faults ("input loss of signal"). Unpowered, the rack passes no line through. Drains optical.amplifierDrain (default 1) FE/t. No panel. See Optical networking.

Crafting and compute#

Compute Server#

Compute Server (2U) gives rack.computeServerThreads (default 2) crafting threads to its network's rack Scheduler. Every Compute Server and Memory Server on the network, in any rack, pools into one Scheduler. It also runs up to elcl.computeServerJobs (default 4) Terminal OS batch jobs at once. Drains rack.computeServerDrain (default 6) FE/t. Its lights blink while the pool uses it and stay solid while it's idle. The panel shows what it gives, what the pool uses of it, whether the Scheduler is active, and the pool's jobs with a cancel button. See Schedulers.

Memory Server#

Memory Server (1U) gives rack.memoryServerMemory (default 32,768) job memory to the rack Scheduler, pooled as above. Drains rack.memoryServerDrain (default 3) FE/t. Same panel as the Compute Server.

Fabrication Server#

Fabrication Server (2U) is a Fabricator in a rack: nine Crafting Schematic slots and two Throughput Module slots. Any Scheduler on its network offers it crafts, the rack Scheduler first. Each craft costs autocrafting.fabricatorEnergyPerCraft (default 50) FE and takes autocrafting.fabricatorCraftTicks (default [20, 10, 5]) ticks with 0, 1 or 2 Throughput Modules. Drains rack.fabricationServerDrain (default 4) FE/t. The panel has the schematic and module slots, progress and the current craft.

Monitoring Server#

Monitoring Server (1U) samples its network once a second: item flow (items in or out of storage a minute), energy use (FE/t), lane use, and crafting jobs finished a minute. It keeps a day of history, saved with the device: a sample a second for the last 10 minutes, one every 10 seconds for the last hour, and one every 2 minutes for the last day. Its panel graphs any of the four over 1 minute, 10 minutes, 1 hour or 1 day, with the maximum and current values. It also keeps (unsaved) series for each machine with a Small Wireless Bridge, for Display Panel graphs. Drains rack.monitoringServerDrain (default 2) FE/t.

Storage#

NAS#

NAS (2U) holds six Storage Drives as network storage, with a priority (-999 to 999; Shift steps by 10) and an access mode (read-write, read-only or write-only), like a Drive Bay. Drains rack.nasDrain (default 2) FE/t plus rack.rackDriveDrain (default 0.5) per drive. See NAS and SAN.

SAN#

SAN (4U) holds 24 Storage Drives, reached through Optical Transceivers in its four rear uplink cages. With no transceiver it faults ("Offline - no uplink") and its drives can't be reached. Each transceiver beyond the first adds one to its storage priority; transceivers don't make it faster. Drains rack.sanDrain (default 4) FE/t plus rack.rackDriveDrain per drive. See NAS and SAN.

4U and 6U Tape Libraries#

4U Tape Library (4U: 24 tape slots, 2 drive bays) and 6U Tape Library (6U: 48 tape slots, 4 drive bays) hold LTO tapes as the network's cold tier, archiving items nobody has touched for a while and recalling them when asked. They need LTO Tape Drives in their drive bays to read and write. Drains rack.tapeLibrary4uDrain (default 3) or rack.tapeLibrary6uDrain (default 4) FE/t, plus rack.tapeDriveBusyDrain (default 8) per busy drive and rack.tapePickerDrain (default 4) while the picker moves. Panel tabs: Magazine and Policy. See LTO tapes and Tape Libraries.

Terminals#

Rack Console, Rack Fabrication Console, Rack Schematic Console#

Rack Console, Rack Fabrication Console and Rack Schematic Console (1U each) are terminals in a drawer. They must be at least 8U apart. Each drains rack.rackConsoleDrain (default 1) FE/t, plus rack.rackConsoleOpenDrain (default 2) while open. No panel. See Rack Consoles.

Modular chassis#

Three large devices are built up from cards, blades and modules you put in their bays. They drain optical.chassisDrain (default 10) FE/t, plus optical.chassisCardDrain (default 2) for each part fitted. All three are on Modular chassis.
