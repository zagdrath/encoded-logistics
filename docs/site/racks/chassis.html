Modular chassis

The Chassis Switch, Blade Enclosure and Optical Transport Shelf, their bays, and all 18 cards, blades and modules that go in them.

On this page
  1. Working with parts
  2. Chassis Switch
  3. Blade Enclosure
  4. Optical Transport Shelf
  5. The Terminal OS

Three rack devices are modular chassis: big enclosures you build up from parts. Each has bays, and each bay takes one kind of part. The chassis works with whatever is fitted, and you can add or pull parts while it runs: taking one out affects only what that part gave.

ChassisHeightBays
Chassis Switch10U2 supervisor bays (A, B), 8 line card bays (L1-L8), 4 power bays (P1-P4), plus 4 transceiver cages behind each Uplink Card
Blade Enclosure10U16 blade bays in front (F1-F16); at the back 2 interconnect bays (R1, R2), 2 management bays (M1, M2), 6 power bays (P1-P6); plus 6 drive bays behind each blade bay and 9 schematic slots for each Fabrication Blade (144 in all)
Optical Transport Shelf6U2 controller bays (C1, C2) and 8 line card bays (L1-L8)

  • Putting a part in: with the rack's front door open, use the part on its bay. Or open the chassis in the rack screen and click a bay while holding the part, or shift-click the part in your inventory to put it in the first bay that takes it.
  • Taking a part out: sneak-click its bay in the chassis panel.
  • Settings: some parts have settings (a Transponder Card's channel, a Protection Card's revertive switch). Click the part in the panel to change them. A part keeps its settings on its item when you take it out.
  • Looking at it: each part's faceplate is drawn in its bay, lit while it works. Look at one through the open door and the popup shows that part: its status and what it gives (or why it's down).
  • Status: each bay has a status word that the Terminal OS's WRKHW screen and RTVCHSSTS report: *ONLINE, *ACTIVE, *STANDBY, *FAILED, *EMPTY, *OFFLINE or *PROTECT.
  • Permissions: putting parts in and taking them out needs Rack access; changing a part's settings needs Build; looking needs View.
  • Power: a chassis drains optical.chassisDrain (default 10) FE/t, plus optical.chassisCardDrain (default 2) FE/t for each part fitted.

The panel's first tab, Slots, draws the chassis's face at full size; you click bays there. Each chassis adds tabs of its own, below.

Chassis Switch#

The Chassis Switch is an L3 Switch built from cards, for racks that need more lanes, more routing throughput, or redundancy.

  • It runs with at least one Supervisor Card and one Chassis Power Supply. It is degraded (amber, "Degraded") with fewer than two of either.
  • Supervisors (bays A and B): the active one runs the switch. If it's taken out, the standby takes over in the same tick, traffic and all, and the Terminal OS gets a *SUPFAILOVER event. With no supervisor left it gets *CARDFAULT.
  • Lanes: each Access Card adds optical.accessCardLanes (default 48) lanes to the rack's pool, like an L2 Switch. The pool only works while the switch runs and at least one Uplink Card cage holds an Optical Transceiver; with none, the pool is 0.
  • Uplinks: each Uplink Card has four Optical Transceiver cages (the Cages tab). They are the pool's uplinks, aggregated: any one keeps the pool up.
  • Routing: with at least one Routing Card the switch has the L3 Switch's routes and QoS (the Switch tab, the same as an L3 Switch's panel). Its rate is the number of Routing Cards times optical.routingCardRate (default 128) items a second.
  • VLAN trunks: a port can be set to trunk mode only with a Trunk Card installed. Without one the switch says so and refuses.
  • Taking out a power supply also sends *CARDFAULT.

Blade Enclosure#

The Blade Enclosure packs compute, memory, fabrication and storage into one 10U chassis. It's the late-game way to fill a rack: each blade is crafted from its server and gives more than that server does, in a fraction of the space.

A full enclosure of Compute Blades gives 64 threads in 10U at the defaults; four of them fill a rack with 256, against 42 from a rack of Compute Servers. A full enclosure of Storage Blades holds 96 drives. The chassis itself drains optical.chassisDrain (default 10) FE/t and each rear module optical.chassisCardDrain (default 2) FE/t.

  • It runs with at least one Interconnect Module, one Management Module and one Blade Power Supply. It is degraded with fewer than two interconnects or two power supplies.
  • Lanes: the blades take none of the rack's lanes. The Interconnect Module gives them optical.interconnectLanes (default 64); a second one is a spare. The Management Module's second is a standby.
  • Compute Blades each give the network's rack Scheduler optical.computeBladeThreads threads.
  • Memory Blades each give it optical.memoryBladeMemory job memory.
  • Fabrication Blades craft as a Fabrication Server with both Throughput Modules does: the last (fastest) of autocrafting.fabricatorCraftTicks, 5 ticks a craft at the defaults, and autocrafting.fabricatorEnergyPerCraft FE a craft.
    • Schematics: each blade has a page of nine schematic slots in the Fab tab, wherever it sits in front: the first blade the first page, the second the second, up to 16 pages. Schematics on a page with no blade wait.
    • Crafts at once: a job normally keeps autocrafting.threadCrafts crafts in flight for each of its threads. Each Fabrication Blade also takes optical.fabricationBladeCrafts crafts beyond that (extra runs), shared by every job on the network. At the defaults a blade crafts as fast as four threads, as a Compute Blade has.
  • Storage Blades each open the six drive bays behind them (the Drives tab, two of each blade's bays a page) as network storage, with a priority and access mode like a NAS.

The panel's tabs: Front, Rear, Drives and Fab. The scheduler pooling is explained on Schedulers.

Optical Transport Shelf#

The Optical Transport Shelf carries several networks between two shelves in different racks, over long Fiber Cable runs, with protection against a cut fibre. Pair two shelves with a Link Card (sneak-use it on one shelf's unit, then use it on the other's); they must be in the same dimension and in different racks.

  • It runs with a Controller Card. The second Controller Card takes over if the first is taken out.
  • Transponder Cards each carry one client network onto one long-haul channel, 1 to 40 (the card's setting). Give a transponder its network with a Link Card: store the network by sneak-using the card on a cable or device of that network, then use it on the transponder's bay. Channel n at one shelf joins its network with channel n's at the other, carrying up to a Fiber Cable's lanes less the line's span loss.
  • Mux Cards: without one the line carries a single channel. Each Mux Card lets it carry 8, up to optical.shelfChannels (default 40) in all. In practice the eight line bays are the limit: every channel needs its own Transponder Card, and the Mux, Amplifier and Protection Cards share the same bays.
  • Amplifier Cards launch the line amplified: a shelf end with one (one counts per end) adds optical.fiberSpanThreshold blocks of reach before the span starts losing lanes.
  • Protection Cards give 1+1 protection: a working path (the shortest fibre route) and a protect path, which leaves the rack from a different connection point by a different fibre (a Network Bridge pair on the way also counts). When the working path fails the shelf switches to protect within a tick (*PROTSWITCH). With the card's revertive setting on (the default), it switches back once the working path is restored. CHGPROT switches by hand, for testing. Without a Protection Card the shelf uses the working path only.
  • Performance data for each path: its length, loss, lanes delivered, signal quality (100 % less 4 % for each lane lost, 0 when down) and protection state, in the Spans tab and the Terminal OS's DSPOPT and RTVOPTSTS.
  • It shows degraded while it's short of redundancy.

The panel's tabs: Slots, Channels and Spans. The optical model (span loss, amplifiers, lines) is explained on Optical networking.

The Terminal OS#

Work with Hardware (WRKHW) lists every chassis and bay on the network with its status. Display Optical (DSPOPT) shows the shelves' and multiplexers' spans and channels. Programs can read them with RTVCHSSTS and RTVOPTSTS, and react to *CARDFAULT, *SUPFAILOVER, *PROTSWITCH and *SPANLOSS trigger events (see Jobs, scheduling and triggers).
