Storage

How a network stores items, fluids, gases and energy on drives, and how it moves what nobody uses out to tape.

A network keeps what you put into it on Storage Drives. A drive is an item: you craft it from a Storage Die, put it in a drive holder on the network, and from then on everything the network stores goes onto the drives in its holders. Take a drive out and its contents go with it; put it back, or into another holder, and they come back.

There are four kinds of drive, one for each resource the network handles (items, fluids, pressurized gases and energy), in five sizes from 8K to 2M. All of them use the same byte model, so a die of a given size makes a drive of the same capacity whatever it stores.

Hot and cold#

Storage comes in two tiers:

  • Hot storage is what the network can hand you at once: the drives in its Drive Bays, Disk Drives, NAS and SAN rack devices and Blade Enclosure Storage Blades, plus any inventories its Inventory Taps face.
  • Cold storage is tape: LTO tapes in Tape Libraries, and the Tape Reel on a Midrange Tape Drive. A Tape Library moves items nobody has touched for a while from the drives onto tape, and brings them back (a recall) when you ask for them.

Terminals show both tiers together, so you never have to know where an item is. Taking more of an item than is hot starts a recall of the rest. The crafting planner counts what's on tape as stored, too.
