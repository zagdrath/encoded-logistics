NAS and SAN

The NAS and SAN rack devices as storage - their drive bays, priority, access mode and the SAN's uplinks.

The NAS and SAN are rack devices that hold Storage Drives, as a Drive Bay does, but with a priority and an access mode you can set. They go in a Server Rack. This page covers how they behave as storage; installing them, their power and lanes, and their panels as rack devices are on Rack devices.

DeviceSizeDrivesDrain
NAS2U6rack.nasDrain (default 2) FE/t, plus rack.rackDriveDrain (default 0.5) FE/t per drive
SAN4U24, plus 4 rear uplink cagesrack.sanDrain (default 4) FE/t, plus rack.rackDriveDrain (default 0.5) FE/t per drive

Both take Storage Drives of any tier and kind. Item, fluid and gas drives are hot storage; Energy Storage Drives in them are part of the network's energy pool. Drives go in through the device's panel. A drive's contents go with it when it comes out, as with any holder.

Priority and access mode#

The device's panel has its drive slots, a capacity bar, and two settings that apply to all its drives:

  • Priority, -999 to 999 (default 0). Higher is filled first and emptied last. Type it and press Enter, or use the steppers (Shift steps by 10).
  • Access mode: read-write, read-only (the network only takes out) or write-only (it only puts in).

How priorities and modes order the network's storage, against Drive Bays (always priority 0) and Inventory Taps, is on Hot and cold storage.

Both settings travel with the device: take it out of the rack and the item keeps them.

The SAN's uplinks#

A SAN is reached through its four rear uplink cages, each taking an Optical Transceiver.

  • No transceiver: the SAN shows FAULT, "Offline - no uplink", and the network can't reach any of its drives.
  • One transceiver: it works at its set priority.
  • Each transceiver beyond the first adds +1 to its storage priority. With all four it stores at its setting + 3.

Its popup and panel show the uplink count.

Storage Blades#

A Blade Enclosure holds drives too: each Storage Blade in it opens two drive bays, which behave as a NAS's do, with the enclosure's priority and access mode. See Modular chassis.
