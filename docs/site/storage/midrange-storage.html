Midrange storage

The Midrange line's Disk Drive and Tape Drive and the Tape Reel - one drive as hot storage, one reel as cold storage, from early materials.

The Midrange line (see The Midrange line) has its own storage peripherals. They're built from Circuit Substrate, a Logic Die, Ferrite and vanilla materials, so they're within reach as soon as you have a Lithography Press, long before a Server Rack. Neither needs a Midrange System: each is an ordinary network device with one lane.

BlockDevice nameHoldsTier
Disk DriveDISK01One Storage DriveHot
Tape DriveTAPE01One Tape ReelCold

Both open a green screen of their own when you use them. Like the other Midrange screens it's text only, with a command line that runs Terminal OS commands as a Terminal Desk's does (see Using the terminal).

Disk Drive#

A Disk Drive holds one Storage Drive, of any tier and any kind, and serves it to its network exactly as a Drive Bay slot does: the same capacity, priority 0, the same fill rules. It's one block, takes its lane from any side but its front, and passes lanes on.

  • Loading: use a drive on it. It spins up for rack.diskSpinUpTicks (default 40) ticks before the network can read it; the lamp lights while the pack spins.
  • Unloading: sneak-use it with an empty hand, or 4=Unload on its screen. It spins down for rack.diskSpinDownTicks (default 20) ticks, then the drive comes out to you (or out of its front, if you've gone).
  • Its screen (DSKDRV): the drive and its state; 4=Unload pack, 5=Display contents.

It drains rack.diskDriveDrain (default 1.5) FE a tick while its network runs.

Tape Drive#

A Tape Drive is a 2.5-block-tall cabinet on a 1 × 3 footprint (place it with two free blocks above). It holds one Tape Reel as cold storage, in the same tier as the Tape Libraries' tapes: what's on the reel shows in terminals, and taking it starts a recall. Its lane is on the bottom block, from any side but the front.

  • Loading: use a reel on it. It threads the reel for rack.tapeLoadTicks (default 100) ticks before it works.
  • Recalls: a waiting recall of an item on its reel is read back into hot storage.
  • Archiving: every rack.tapeArchiveInterval (default 200) ticks, while the network's item drives are fuller than rack.tapeDefaultHotPercent (default 80) %, it writes the item type untouched longest (for at least rack.tapeDefaultAgeHours (default 2) hours) onto its reel. It uses the config's values directly: it has no policy screen, keep-hot or pinned list of its own.
  • Timing: a read or write takes rack.tapeDriveBaseTicks (default 60) ticks plus rack.tapeDriveTicksPer4k (default 20) ticks for every 4,096 items. The lamps on top light while it works.
  • 7=Rewind rewinds the reel to its load point (rack.tapeRewindTicks (default 40) ticks), stopping whatever it was doing. 4=Unload, or a sneak-use with an empty hand, rewinds first, then the reel comes out to you (or out of its front).
  • Its screen (TAPDRV): its state and reel; 4=Unload, 5=Display contents (most first), 7=Rewind.

It drains rack.tapeDriveDrain (default 2) FE a tick, twice that while reading or writing.

Tape Reel#

A Tape Reel holds rack.tapeReelItems (default 65,536) items of up to rack.tapeReelTypes (default 1024) types. Unlike drives and LTO tapes, a reel reserves no room per type: it's a plain item count. Its tooltip shows the fill ("18,420 / 65,536 items") and its volume serial (VOL001 to VOL999).

As with drives and LTO tapes, the contents are kept in the world's save under the reel's id, given the first time it's mounted, so a reel keeps what's on it out of the drive. Reels don't stack.

Note

Tape Reels go only in a Tape Drive. Tape Libraries take LTO tapes only. When you outgrow a reel, move up to LTO tapes and a Tape Library.
