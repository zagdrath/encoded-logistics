LTO tapes and Tape Libraries

LTO tapes and the LTO Tape Drive, the 4U and 6U Tape Libraries, the archiving policy, recalls, upgrading tapes, and what CHGITMTIER does.

On this page

  2. Upgrading tapes

  5. The archiving policy
  6. How archiving works
  7. Recalls
  8. Timing

Tape is a network's cold storage: far more room than drives, for items nobody is using. A Tape Library in a Server Rack holds LTO tapes and the drives that read and write them. On its own it moves item types that haven't been touched for a while from the network's drives onto tape, and brings them back when they're wanted. You keep seeing them in terminals the whole time; the only difference is the moment a recall takes.

For how cold storage fits with the rest of the network's storage, see Hot and cold storage. Installing a library in a rack, and its power and lanes, are on Rack devices.

LTO tapes#

There are five generations. Each holds four times the one before and reads and writes 15% faster:

TapeBytesItems, one typeRead/write time
LTO-6 Tape8,388,608 (8M)66,584,576× 1
LTO-7 Tape33,554,432 (32M)266,338,304× 0.85
LTO-8 Tape134,217,728 (128M)1,065,353,216× 0.72
LTO-9 Tape536,870,912 (512M)4,261,412,864× 0.61
LTO-10 Tape2,147,483,648 (2G)17,045,651,456× 0.52

Tapes use the same byte model as Storage Drives: 8 items a byte, each type reserving a 128th of the tape, and at most storage.driveTypeLimit (default 63) types a tape. Tapes hold items only.

They behave like drives as items, too. A new tape stacks to 64; the first time it goes into a library it gets an id, its contents are kept in the world's save under that id, and from then on it stacks to 1. A tape taken out keeps what's on it until it goes back into a library; one that's empty again goes back to blank in your inventory.

Upgrading tapes#

Each generation is crafted from the one before. The new tape takes the old one's id, so it keeps everything on it: you can upgrade a full tape.

ToOld tape plus
LTO-72 Ferrite, a Logic Die
LTO-82 Neodymium Ingots, a Memory Die
LTO-92 Neodymium Ingots, a Tantalum Capacitor
LTO-102 Gallium Ingots, a Processor Die

The new tape's fill is worked out again once it's in a library.

The LTO Tape Drive#

An LTO Tape Drive goes in one of a library's drive bays; it stacks to 16. A library's tapes are part of the network's cold storage while the library is online, drive or no drive, but reading or writing a tape needs a drive. With no drive the library can't archive or recall anything.

The Tape Libraries#

LibrarySizeTape slotsDrive baysIdle drain
4U Tape Library4U242rack.tapeLibrary4uDrain (default 3) FE/t
6U Tape Library6U484rack.tapeLibrary6uDrain (default 4) FE/t

On top of the idle drain, each drive reading or writing adds rack.tapeDriveBusyDrain (default 8) FE/t and the picker rack.tapePickerDrain (default 4) FE/t while it moves. Libraries take LTO tapes only, in their tape slots, and LTO Tape Drives in their bays.

The library's panel has two tabs:

  • Magazine: the tape slots (eight to a row, three rows at a time; scroll for the rest), a fill bar under each tape, the drive bays and what each drive is doing, the cold capacity bar, and what the library is doing now.
  • Policy: the archiving rules below.

On its front you can watch the picker robot carry tapes between the slots and the drives, with its own sounds.

The archiving policy#

Set on the library's Policy tab:

SettingWhat it doesDefault
Archive afterHow long an item type must go untouched on the drives before it's archived: 1 to 9,999 minutes, hours or days (game time: an hour is 72,000 ticks)2 hours
Only while hot storage is aboveThe free-space trigger: archive only while the network's item drives are fuller than this % (0 to 100). It can be turned off, and then age alone decidesOn, 80 %
Keep hotNine filter entries (right-click one for its fuzzy match). Items that match are never archivedEmpty
Pinned (always hot)Nine items that are never archived, and are recalled by themselves if any of them is found on tapeEmpty

Click an entry with an item to set it, or empty-handed to clear it; you can drag items from JEI onto them. The tab also shows how full hot storage is now. The defaults for new libraries come from the config (rack.tapeDefaultAgeHours, rack.tapeDefaultHotPercent).

How archiving works#

Every rack.tapeArchiveInterval (default 200) ticks, a library with an idle drive and at least one tape looks for work:

  1. If the trigger is on and the item drives aren't fuller than its percentage, it does nothing.
  2. Of several libraries on one network, only the one with the most free tape space archives (the first, on a tie).
  3. It lists the item types on the network's drives untouched for longer than its age, leaving out keep-hot matches, pinned items, and anything being recalled. Oldest first.
  4. Each idle drive takes one type: the picker loads a tape, the drive writes, the picker puts the tape back. A type goes on the tape that already holds some of it, if that has room; otherwise on the fullest tape with room, so tapes fill one at a time.

If the item is touched (put in or taken out) while its tape is being loaded, it stays hot. Only item drives are archived: fluids, gases and what's in Inventory Taps' inventories stay where they are.

Recalls#

A recall brings an item back from tape into hot storage: when someone takes more than is hot, when a crafting job needs it, or for a pinned item. Recalls queue oldest first (asking again for an item already waiting takes the larger amount). A library with an idle drive takes the oldest waiting recall for an item on its tapes, from the tape holding most of it, one tape a drive. Recalls go before archiving.

If hot storage fills during a recall, what doesn't fit stays on tape and the item shows "hot storage full" until it's asked for again.

Timing#

A read or write takes rack.tapeBaseTicks (default 40) ticks plus rack.tapeTicksPer4k (default 20) ticks for every 4,096 items, times the tape's speed from the table above, plus the picker's trip to and from the drive. So 4,096 items take 60 ticks on LTO-6 and 31 on LTO-10, before the picker. Terminals and the plan screen show how long a recall will take, counting the queue ahead of it.

CHGITMTIER#

CHGITMTIER sets how an item is tiered, from the Terminal OS or a script. There is no per-item tier of its own: the command works through the Tape Libraries' keep-hot and pinned lists. It first takes the item off every library's lists on the network, then:

TIERWhat it does
*HOTAdds it to the first library's keep-hot list with a free entry, and recalls everything of it that's on tape
*PINAdds it to the first library's pinned list with a free entry (so it's recalled whenever it's found on tape)
*COLDHas the first online library archive it on its next go, whatever its age or the trigger (only what's on the drives; a restart forgets the request)
*AUTONothing more: the archiving rules decide
CHGITMTIER ITEM(COBBLESTONE) TIER(*COLD)
CHGITMTIER ITEM(LOGIC_DIE) TIER(*PIN)

It fails with ELC1206 when no Tape Library serves the network (a Midrange Tape Drive doesn't count), and with ELC1305 when every list it could go on is full. It needs the Firewall's BUILD permission (ELCL's CONFIGURE). You see the result on the libraries' Policy tabs.
