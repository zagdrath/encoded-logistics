Drive Bay

The Drive Bay, a block that holds ten Storage Drives and makes them part of its network's storage.

The Drive Bay is the first drive holder you'll build, and the one most networks are built around. It holds ten Storage Drives of any tier and any kind (items, fluids, gases, energy), and while it's on a running network they're part of that network's storage.

Setting one up#

  1. Place it. It faces you, and cables connect to it on every face except the front.
  2. Cable it to the network. It's a network device and uses one lane (see Lanes).
  3. Open it and put drives in its slots. The drives are part of the network's storage at once.

The Drive Bay drains storage.driveBayDrain (default 2) FE a tick while its network runs, plus storage.driveBayDrainPerDrive (default 0.5) FE a tick for each drive in it.

The screen and the front#

The screen lays out the ten slots as the front does, two columns of five. Each drive has a fill bar beside it in its light's colour (green, yellow, orange, red: see fill lights), and the title strip says Online or Offline.

On the block, each drive shows as a sled in its slot, coloured by tier, with its status light. The lights go dark while the bay is offline (no power, no lane, or no controller).

How the network uses it#

  • Item, fluid and gas drives in a Drive Bay are hot storage at priority 0, read and write. A Drive Bay has no priority or access setting of its own: for those, use a NAS or SAN or an Inventory Tap. See Hot and cold storage for how priorities order the network's storage.
  • Energy drives in a Drive Bay are part of the network's energy pool, not storage. See Energy drives as batteries.

Taking a drive out (or breaking the bay) takes its contents with it: they live under the drive's id, not in the bay. Put the drive in any holder and they're back.

Tip

One Drive Bay of 8K drives is plenty to start with. When you run short, swap drives for bigger ones rather than adding bays: a 32K drive holds four times an 8K and costs the same lane and drain.
