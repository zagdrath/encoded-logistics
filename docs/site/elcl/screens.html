Inventory, crafting jobs, devices and network status#

These are the desk's own screens, Main Menu options 1–4. They work on the network's storage and machines rather than on the OS. They use the desk's own authority check: without the Firewall permission they need they say Not authorised to ... on the message line.

Work with Inventory (WRKINV)#

The network's items, fluids and gases, hot (on drives) and cold (on tape; items only). Columns: Opt, Item, Type (ITEM, FLUID, PRES), Quantity (fluids and gases in B / mB), Location (Hot, Cold, Hot+Cold). After what's stored, the list has what the network can craft but has none of (a Processing Schematic's or a diskette recipe's output), dim, with Quantity 0 and Location Craftable: 7=Craft starts it there, and 1=Withdraw answers None of Chest stored: 7=Craft makes some. Position to takes the starting characters of a name (blank or *ALL: all); Type keeps one type (*ITEM, *FLUID, *PRES; *ALL, the default: all). The FILTER() parameter is accepted on a command line but the screen opens unfiltered; use Position to. Clicking a row types 5.

  • 1=Withdraw opens the Withdraw Item screen: Item, Quantity 1–999999, Destination *DRAWER (the desk drawer, the default) or *INV (your inventory); it shows what's on hand and how long a tape recall would take. Withdrawing a fluid or gas (its Item reads FLUID minecraft:water, its Quantity in mB, a bucket to start with) fills empty buckets, tanks or gas containers at the destination.
  • 5=Display details: item id, name, hot and cold counts, recall time, whether it's craftable, tags.
  • 7=Craft opens the Craft Item screen: Item, Quantity, Scheduler (*AUTO, a number or a name), Destination (*NETWORK, *DRAWER, *INV) and a one-line plan: steps, missing, recall time.
  • F6 opens Deposit Items: what's in your inventory (the hotbar's slots, then the rest); 1=Deposit puts that stack into the network, F6 everything outside your hotbar (it needs the Firewall's insert permission). What doesn't fit stays with you.
  • F11 sorts by name, quantity or mod.

At the Integrated Midrange System's console, which has no drawer, Withdraw goes to your inventory and Craft to the network. Keys: F1=Help F3=Exit F5=Refresh F6=Deposit F9=Command Entry F11=Sort F12=Cancel. See Terminal Desk and Swivel Chair for the drawer.

Work with Jobs (WRKCRFJOB)#

The network's crafting jobs (separate from the OS's script jobs on Work with Active Jobs). Columns: Opt, Job, Item, Qty, Status (Active, Waiting, Recall), Progress (bar and %), Scheduler. It refreshes every two seconds. Clicking a row types 5.

  • 4=Cancel: the message line asks you to confirm; press Enter again.
  • 5=Display: the job (Display Job).
  • 8=Display steps: Display Job Steps, a tree of the job's steps with each one's state and done/total, and a stalled step's reason in bright under it.

F10=History switches to the jobs that ended, the crafting job history (see Crafting jobs), and F10 again back to the active ones. The history's title is Crafting Job History; Position to at the top keeps the jobs whose item starts with what's typed. Columns: Opt, Job, Item, Qty (asked for), Status (Done, Failed bright, Cancelled), Ended (game day and time), Duration, Requested by (the player's user, or the ELCL job's name). Newest first; F11 sorts by ended time, item or status. Options: 4=Remove (confirmed with the others; it takes the Firewall's craft permission, as cancelling does), 5=Display (Display Ended Job: the item and quantity asked for and made, status and failure reason, who asked, the scheduler that ran it, when it started and ended, how long it ran, and the ingredients it used up from storage), 7=Craft again (the Craft Item screen with the same item and quantity, while the network can still craft it).

Work with Devices (WRKDEV)#

The network as a topology, each device once. First each Server Rack (Type Rack, its lanes on the network), with the devices in it listed under it on tree lines, top unit first (├─U39-42, └─U1-2). Then everything that isn't in a rack: a controller structure, Drive Bays, terminals, parts on cables (Part), Control Interfaces and other devices, Access Points, Wireless Bridges and Wireless Ports (their Status names the Wireless Controller they work through, Online WLC01, or what's wrong: No controller, No access points, Over capacity). Cables and a rack's other blocks aren't listed. Columns: Opt, Type, Device, Location, Lanes, Status (Online, Offline dim, a fault or what it's busy with bright). The Device column starts with the device's ELCL name (UPS01, INGRESS02, CTLIF01) when it has one: these are the names programs use. Clicking a row types 5.

  • 2=Change: rename it (a window for the new name, which runs RNMDEV; Firewall build permission; ELC1309 when done, ELC1308 if the name is in use).
  • 5=Display: device, name, location, units, lanes, status (and for wireless gear, its clients, controller, uplink, devices, lanes, inventory and what it moved).
  • 8=Locate: a box blinks around it in the world for ten seconds (a rack device's own units, or its block).

Display Network Status (DSPNETSTS)#

Storage (hot and cold; fluid and pressurized drives on their own lines when there are any, in buckets), energy (stored, the part in Energy Storage Drives when there are any, in / out), lanes used, crafting (active jobs) and devices online, with gauges. The bytes the system's source members and files take count as used hot storage here (as they do for RTVSTGSTS and the *STGFULL trigger). It refreshes every two seconds; Enter returns. Keys: F1=Help F3=Exit F5=Refresh F12=Cancel.

OptionWhat it does
2=EditOpens the source editor.
3=CopyA window asking for To library and To member; runs CPYMBR. Copying from ELSYS suggests your current library.
4=DeleteConfirmed, then DLTMBR.
5=DisplayThe editor, read-only (title Display Member; only FIND, TOP and BOTTOM on its command line).

7=RenameA window asking for the new name; runs RNMMBR.

9 and 16 need the member's program, compiled since the source last changed. A member with no program says Program LIB/MEMBER is not compiled. on the message line, and one marked Chg * says Member LIB/MEMBER changed since it was compiled.; either way a Compile First window offers to compile it: Enter runs 14=Compile and, once ELC0218 comes back, opens the prompter as if the program had been there (a compile that fails, ELC0206, stops there); F12 leaves it. On a PF member they answer Option 9 not valid for this item. (there's nothing to run).

In ELSYS only 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 14 (into a library you can change) and 16 work; 2, 4 and 7 give ELC0205 (Library ELSYS is read-only.). F6 prompts CRTMBR with the library filled in. F11 sorts by name, by changed-since-compile, or by text.

Work with Machines (WRKMCH)#

The network's Arcforge machines (Main Menu option 9): each machine with a linked Small Wireless Bridge on it. Columns: Machine (its device name, ARCCRU01), Type (the machine's own name), Status (*IDLE, *RUNNING, *NOPOWER, *NOINPUT, *BLOCKED, *DISABLED, *NOTFORMED, *FAULT, or *OFFLINE while the bridge isn't on the network or the machine is gone; bright when it needs attention), Prog (progress through the current operation), Energy (FE stored / capacity) and Rate (operations a minute over the last minute). How option lists work#

  • Each row has a 3-character Opt field in column 0. Type option codes next to as many rows as you like, on any page; they stay with their rows when you page.
  • Enter takes them top to bottom. Options that open a window or a screen wait until you're done with it, then the next one runs.
  • Delete-type options (4=Delete, 4=End, 4=Remove) are gathered and confirmed together in one window, after the other options have run: Press Enter to confirm your choices for 4=Delete, F12 to return. followed by the rows. (Display Messages' 4=Remove doesn't ask, and Work with Jobs' 4=Cancel asks on the message line.)
  • An option the screen doesn't have gives Option N not valid for this item.: nothing runs, and the cursor goes to that row so you can correct it.
  • Commands an option runs come back with their message on the message line, and the list refreshes.
  • Clicking a row types the screen's default option (5=Display on most screens, 2 where changing is the usual thing).
