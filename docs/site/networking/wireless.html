Wireless

The Wireless Controller and its Access Points, Wireless Bridges, Wireless Ingress and Egress Ports, the Radio Module, and the cross-dimension options.

On this page
  1. The parts
  2. Wireless Controller
  3. Access Points
  4. Linking clients
  5. Wireless Bridge
  6. Wireless Ingress and Egress Ports
  7. Across dimensions
  8. In the Terminal OS

Wireless lets you put parts of a network where no cable goes: a Wireless Bridge brings a whole remote area onto your network, and a Wireless Port works an inventory with no cable at all. Range is unlimited within a dimension, and other dimensions can be reached too.

There are two separate wireless systems in the mod, and it's worth knowing which is which:

SystemServesRange
Relay AntennasHandheld Terminals onlyA sphere around each antenna, same dimension
The Wireless Controller with Access Points (this page)Wireless Bridges, Wireless Ports and Small Wireless Bridges; also Handheld TerminalsUnlimited in the dimension; other dimensions while rack.wirelessCrossDimension (default true)

The parts#

PartWhat it is
Wireless ControllerA 1U rack device: the network's wireless brain. Clients link to it
Access PointA radio on the network. Each one online gives the controller room for rack.wirelessApClients (default 8) clients
Wireless BridgeA client: whatever is cabled to it joins the controller's network
Wireless Ingress Port, Wireless Egress PortClients: an Ingress or Egress Port with no cable
Radio ModuleA crafting component for all of the above

A typical setup: a Wireless Controller in a rack on your main network, two or three Access Points cabled to that network, and Wireless Bridges and Ports out in the world, each linked to the controller with a Link Card.

Wireless Controller#

The Wireless Controller is a 1U device for a Server Rack. It drains rack.wirelessControllerDrain (default 4) FE/t and uses one lane.

  • Clients (Wireless Bridges, Wireless Ports, Small Wireless Bridges) link to it with a Link Card, below.
  • Handheld Terminals link to it by being used on its unit or anywhere on its rack; they then work anywhere. See Handheld Terminal.
  • Admission: the controller admits clients up to the slots its network's online Access Points give (rack.wirelessApClients each), oldest link first. Clients past the slots wait, "Over capacity", until slots free up.
  • Lanes: each admitted client is a remote link from the controller's rack: a Wireless Bridge carries rack.wirelessBridgeLanes (default 32) lanes, a Wireless Port or Small Wireless Bridge one.
  • Fault: with clients linked and no Access Point online, it faults: "No access points".
  • Popup: Access Points online, clients admitted against slots, and how many are linked.

Its panel has two tabs:

  • APs: each Access Point with its online dot and clients ("6 / 8 clients", "8 / 8 - full", or "no uplink").
  • Linked: its bridges, ports and machines, then the players whose Handheld Terminals it serves, each with an X to unlink it. Unlinking a client here cuts it off.

The controller keeps its list of linked clients and players with it when you take it out of the rack.

While rack.wirelessAnyController (default true), a Handheld Terminal linked through one controller can use any online controller on the network. Clients always belong to the controller they were linked to.

Access Points#

An Access Point is a disc you mount against a block face, usually the side of a cable. It faces away from the face you click and connects only on that one side: it's a device at the end of a cable, not something the network passes through.

  • It uses one lane and drains rack.accessPointDrain (default 2) FE/t.
  • Each one online gives its network's Wireless Controller rack.wirelessApClients (default 8) client slots. Admitted clients are shared out among the online Access Points in turn.
  • States: off (no uplink: not on a running network), linking (on a network with no Wireless Controller: the ring blinks yellow), online (light blue).
  • Popup: its clients (n / 8) and its uplink. Its device name is AP01 and so on.

Access Points don't need to be anywhere near the clients: range is unlimited. They only set how many clients the controller can take. Handheld Terminals don't need them at all.

Linking clients#

  1. Open the controller's rack front door and use a Link Card on the Wireless Controller's unit. The card takes the controller.
  2. Use the card on a Wireless Bridge, a Wireless Port, or a Small Wireless Bridge on a machine. It becomes the controller's client. A card from another controller moves it to that one.

Linking a client needs Build on the controller's network. A client whose controller no longer lists it (unlinked in the panel, say) unlinks itself.

A client's status shows what's wrong, if anything:

StatusMeans
Not linked, No controllerWaiting: not linked yet, or its controller can't be found (shown as linking)
No access pointsFault: its controller's network has no Access Point online
Over capacityFault: the controller has more clients than its Access Points' slots

Clients in another dimension from the controller are only admitted while rack.wirelessCrossDimension is on.

Wireless Bridge#

Once linked, whatever is cabled to a Wireless Bridge (on its sides and bottom; its mast is on top) is on the controller's network, as if a rack.wirelessBridgeLanes-lane cable ran from the controller's rack to it. That makes it a wireless Network Bridge with no partner to place: put it at the outpost, cable the outpost to it, link it.

  • It uses no lanes itself and counts as one client.
  • It drains a Network Bridge's reach.bridgeDrain FE/t times rack.wirelessEnergyMultiplier (default 2).
  • States: off, linking, online, active (lanes crossing: its window pulses), fault.
  • It has no screen. Its popup shows its controller, the devices behind it, and its lanes. Its device name is WBRIDGE01 and so on.

Don't put a second controller structure behind a Wireless Bridge: it joins the controller's network, and a network has one controller structure.

Wireless Ingress and Egress Ports#

A Wireless Port is an Ingress or Egress Port without a cable: a small panel you place against the inventory it works (it faces into it). It pulls items, fluids, gases or energy from that inventory into its controller's network (Ingress) or pushes them out of the network into it (Egress), with the same filter, modules, redstone modes and screen as a cabled port.

  • Its screen's title is the port's name, and a link light and signal bars sit under the redstone button.
  • It connects to no cable. It takes one lane over its link to the controller's rack and counts as one client.
  • It drains storage.partDrain FE/t times rack.wirelessEnergyMultiplier.
  • States: off, linking, online, fault. Device names WINGRESS01 and WEGRESS01.

Handy for a single chest or machine far from any cable: one port, linked, and it's on the network.

Across dimensions#

OptionDefaultControls
rack.wirelessCrossDimensiontrueWireless clients, and Handheld Terminals linked to a controller, in another dimension from the Wireless Controller
rack.wirelessAnyControllertrueA Handheld Terminal using any online controller on its network, not just the one it was linked to
reach.bridgeCrossDimensiontruePairing Network Bridges across dimensions (see Bridges and links)

Relay Antennas always cover only their own dimension.

In the Terminal OS#

Access Points, Wireless Bridges and Wireless Ports appear in Work with Devices with their device names, and the Wireless Controller as WLC01. Like any device they can be renamed with RNMDEV.
