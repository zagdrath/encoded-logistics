Networking

Joining, splitting and connecting networks - switches and VLANs, Routers, exchanges and directories, bridges and links, optical fibre and wireless.

A single network, one controller structure and everything cabled to it, gets you a long way (see Networks). This section is about what comes next: sharing lanes inside a rack, carrying several segments down one cable, moving items between separate networks, and reaching far-off places by fibre, bridge or radio.

Joining versus routing#

Most of the hardware here does one of two quite different things, and it helps to keep them apart.

Joining makes two places one network: the same storage, the same lanes, the same controller. A Network Bridge pair, a Point-to-Point Link carrying lanes, an optical channel or a Wireless Bridge all join: whatever is cabled at the far end becomes part of the network at the near end. A network has exactly one controller structure, so only one of the places you join should have one.

Routing keeps networks separate and moves items between them by rule. Each network keeps its own controller, storage and Firewall. The L3 Switch routes between the segments a rack knows; the Router routes between whole networks, any distance apart, and can learn routes from other Routers.

The Link Card#

Almost everything here is set up with a Link Card: you sneak-use it on one end to store that end, then use it on the other end to pair, link or assign. The card's uses are collected on Bridges and links.

Reading order#

  1. Switches and VLANs: lane pools, segments and VLANs inside a rack.
  2. Routers: moving items between networks.
  3. Exchange Switch and Directory Server: many networks meeting, and reaching devices by name.
  4. Bridges and links: joining places, and the Link Card.
  5. Optical networking: fibre, span loss, multiplexers and transport shelves.
  6. Wireless: Access Points, Wireless Bridges and Ports, the Wireless Controller.
