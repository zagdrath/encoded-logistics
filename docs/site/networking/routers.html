Routers

The Router - linking separate networks with a Link Card, Move and Share routes with filters, static and dynamic routing, transit, the route table and neighbours, and ACLs.

A Router is a 1U rack device at the edge of your network. It moves items between separate networks: networks that each keep their own controller, storage and Firewall, however far apart and in whichever dimension. Your mining outpost, a friend's base, the nether factory: link each to a Router, and set up routes for what should flow where.

A Router is not how you join places into one network (that's a Network Bridge or an optical channel), and it doesn't route between the segments of one rack (that's the L3 Switch). It routes between whole networks.

Linking networks#

A Router's endpoints are its own network (the one its rack is on) plus up to five networks you link to it, six in all.

  1. Sneak-use a Link Card on any block of the other network: a cable, a device, a Drive Bay. The card stores that network. (A Segment Isolator half stores a segment instead, which a Router also accepts.)
  2. Open the front door of the Router's rack and use the card on the Router's unit. The action bar says it's linked, that the Router already knows it, or that the Router is full.

Linking needs Rack access at the Router's rack and Build on the other network. The Router finds a linked network through the block you stored on the card, so a route only works while that block is loaded and on a running network.

In the panel's Routes tab you can rename endpoints (up to 24 characters) and unlink a network, which removes the routes that use it.

Routes#

A Router holds up to 6 routes. A route goes from one endpoint to another (click its source or destination to step through them) and has a mode. Click the mode to step Move, Share, Control.

ModeWhat it does
MoveOnce a second, moves the items its filter passes from the source network's storage into the destination's
ShareMoves nothing. The source's items that pass the filter become part of the destination's storage, as its terminals and crafting see them
ControlMoves nothing and shows nothing. Lets the source network's Terminal OS reach devices the destination's Directory Servers publish (see Exchange Switch and Directory Server)

Share options: Read (see and take out) or Read/Write (put in too, once the destination's own storage is full); whether crafting may use the shared items; and one way or both ways (bidirectional).

Filters. Each route has a 9-entry filter with deny, tag and component options, as a port's has. An empty allow list passes nothing, so a new route moves nothing until you set it up; an empty deny list passes everything. You can drag items from JEI onto a route's filter box.

Throughput. A Router moves up to rack.routerBaseRate (default 16) items a second, plus rack.routerRatePerTransceiver (default 16) for each Optical Transceiver in its three cages, shared between its active Move routes in turn. Each transceiver also adds rack.routerDrainPerTransceiver (default 1) FE/t to its drain of rack.routerDrain (default 4). The Routes tab shows the rate, the items actually moved per second, and which endpoints are reachable now.

Permissions. Rack access is enough to add and edit routes. Removing or retargeting a route that has an ACL, and stepping a route into or out of Control (or out of Share), need Build as well, so nobody with only rack access can get round an ACL.

Static and dynamic routing#

Every Router starts in Static mode: it routes between its own endpoints and nothing more. Switch it to Dynamic in the Mode tab (needs Build) and it takes part in dynamic routing with every other Dynamic Router on the server.

  • Neighbours. Two Dynamic Routers are neighbours when one is linked to the other's network, or both are members peering at an Exchange Switch.
  • Hellos. Each Dynamic Router says hello once a second. A neighbour heard from goes Init, then Full once each has seen the other. One that misses about three hellos goes Down (sooner if needed to reconverge within rack.reconvergeSeconds (default 5) seconds).
  • What it learns. Each Router advertises its network, the networks it links to, and its transit policy. Through its Full neighbours it learns every network beyond them, and works out the cheapest path to each, at most rack.maxHops (default 16) hops long (fewer hops break ties). An advertisement not refreshed for a minute ages out, so an unloaded Router drops off.
  • Cost. Each hop costs more the slower the Routers on it: rack.routingBaseCost (default 100) scaled down by the hop's throughput, so Routers with more transceivers make cheaper paths. In the Mode tab you can raise or lower the cost to a neighbour in steps of 10 to steer traffic, or clear it; CHGNBR does the same.

With dynamic routing a route's destination can be any network in the route table, not just an endpoint: click the destination to step past the endpoints into the table. Items on such a remote route travel hop by hop along the best path, each Router on the way carrying them and spending its own throughput, and never land in a network on the way. A remote route with no path right now pauses (nothing moves, a Share shows nothing) until there's one again.

A Router's own links always win over paths it learns. Its whole throughput, for its own routes and for traffic passing through it, is the one rate above.

Transit#

Whether other networks' traffic may pass through a Router's network is its transit setting, in the Mode tab:

TransitLets through
None (the default)Nothing: its network is only ever the start or end of a path
ListedTraffic from the networks you tick in its route table, up to 16
AllEveryone's traffic

A path crosses a network only where its Routers allow transit for the traffic's source. At an exchange, each member can also allow or refuse transit to each peer.

The route table and neighbours#

The Table tab lists every network the Router can reach:

ColumnShows
NetworkThe destination network's name
NextWhere the path goes first: direct, a network, or IX:name for an exchange
Cost, hopsThe path's cost and length
Source*STATIC (its own Link Card links, always listed first), *CONNECTED (one hop away, through a neighbour), *DYNAMIC (learned, further away)
AgeHow long the entry has been the same

The Mode tab lists the neighbours: their network, how they're reached (a link, or an exchange), their state, and their cost and any override.

In the Terminal OS, DSPROUTE and DSPNBR show the same tables, RTVRTE and RTVNBRSTS read them into a program, and the trigger events *RTECHG, *NBRUP and *NBRDOWN fire when a best path changes or a neighbour comes up or goes down.

ACLs#

An ACL (access control list) limits what a Router lets through. Each Router holds up to 8 named ACLs (names up to 10 letters and digits, starting with a letter) of up to 16 rules each.

Applying an ACL. In the ACLs tab, apply an ACL to a route, or to a network the Router reaches directly (a linked network, a neighbour's, or an exchange peer's). A Move then moves only what the ACLs on its way permit, and a Share shows only what they permit. Creating, editing and applying ACLs needs Build.

How a rule is chosen. The rules are checked in order; the first that matches the resource and its direction decides. If none matches, the default applies: permit while rack.aclDefaultPermit (default true), deny otherwise. Each rule counts its hits and when it last matched.

Rule syntax#

The panel's rule editor takes a rule as one line:

MATCH [permit|deny] [in|out|both] [N/m] [N/d]
PartMeans
MATCHWhat it matches: an item (COAL, minecraft:coal), a tag (#minecraft:logs), a fluid or gas (FLUID minecraft:water), *ITEM, *FLUID, *PRES (gases), *ENERGY, *ANY, or *CONTROL (Directory control requests)
permit / denyWhether it lets the match through. Permit if left out
in / out / bothWhich way: in is into the Router's own network, out is out of it. Both if left out
N/mA rate: at most N a minute. Past it the rule denies until the minute turns
N/dA quota: at most N a game day. Past it the rule denies until the day turns

For example, COAL deny out keeps your coal at home, and *ANY permit out 64/m 2000/d lets anything out but only 64 a minute and 2,000 a day. A rule's rate and quota count what Move routes move; a Share honours them once Moves have used them up.

When a rule denies something, the Terminal OS's *ACLDENY trigger event fires (at most once a second for each rule).

ACLs from the Terminal OS#

WRKACL works with a Router's ACL on screen. ADDACLE, CHGACLE and RMVACLE add, change and remove rules from a program; ADDACLE makes the ACL if it doesn't exist yet. Rules are numbered in tens, so you can insert between them.

/* Keep coal at home, and limit logs leaving to 64 a minute. */
ADDACLE RTR(ROUTER01) ACL(EXPORT) MATCH(COAL) DIR(*OUT) ACTION(*DENY)
ADDACLE RTR(ROUTER01) ACL(EXPORT) MATCH('#minecraft:logs') DIR(*OUT) ACTION(*PERMIT) RATE(64)

You still apply the ACL to a route or network in the Router's ACLs tab.

Exchanges#

A Dynamic Router can join an Exchange Switch with a Cross-Connect: use the Cross-Connect on the Router's unit (needs Build on its network), then on the Exchange Switch. At the exchange it peers with other members' Routers. Exchange peerings show in its route table with IX:name as the next hop.
