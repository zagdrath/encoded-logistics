Terminal Desk and Swivel Chair

The Terminal Desk, its CRT and drawer, and the Swivel Chair - where you sit down to use the Terminal OS.

On this page
  1. Placing it
  2. On the network
  3. Using it
  4. The Swivel Chair

The Terminal Desk is a steel-and-laminate desk with a green-screen CRT and a keyboard. It is the network's command terminal: instead of a storage grid it runs the Terminal OS, where you type commands, look at jobs and devices, and write ELCL programs. This page covers the desk as a block; the OS itself has its own section, starting at Terminal OS and ELCL and Using the terminal.

Placing it#

The desk is two blocks wide and one deep, and faces you when you place it. The left half (seen from the front) holds the CRT and keyboard: that's the part that matters. The right half is a drawer pedestal. Breaking either half breaks both, and the desk drops its drawer's contents.

On the network#

The desk is a network device. Cables connect to it on any side except the front, and it uses one lane. It drains rack.terminalDeskDrain (default 1) FE/t, plus rack.terminalDeskScreenDrain (default 2) FE/t while its screen is on.

The screen follows the network:

  • Offline (no network, no power or no lane): the screen is dark.
  • Coming online: it boots for three seconds, with a degauss thump.
  • Online: the screen stays lit and the CRT hums quietly. Keys clack as you type.

Each desk gets a device name the first time it is on a network, ELDESK01 and so on. You can rename it with RNMDEV or option 2 on Work with Devices.

Using it#

DoAnd you get
Use the CRT halfThe Terminal OS screen
Use the pedestal halfThe desk drawer: 9 slots
Sneak-use either half with an empty handTidies the desk top, or clutters it again (just for looks)

The Terminal OS needs the View permission on a network with a Firewall. It stays open while you are near the desk and the desk is there. It is an 80x24 green screen with its own keys (F3 exits, F4 prompts, F12 or Esc goes back) and command line. All of that is on Using the terminal.

The drawer is where things you withdraw from the Terminal OS to *DRAWER arrive, and where finished crafts can be delivered. Hoppers and pipes can take items out of it through the pedestal half, so you can send withdrawals on elsewhere.

The screen colour (green, amber or white) is the system's PHOSPHOR value, unless you pick your own with the client option terminal.phosphor (default *SYSVAL) or the F24 (Shift+F12) page in the OS.

The console of an Integrated Midrange System opens the same Terminal OS session as a desk; see The Midrange line.

The Swivel Chair#

The Swivel Chair is a five-star office chair to go with the desk. It does nothing for the network; it's for sitting at the desk in style.

  • Placing: it faces the way you are looking, at any angle, not only the four compass directions.
  • Sitting: right-click it to sit down. The seat and back turn with you as you look around. One player at a time.
  • Getting up: sneak. You stand up a block in front of the seat. Breaking the chair stands you up too.
  • Dyeing: put it in a crafting grid with dyes to colour its upholstery, as you would leather armour. Undyed it's brown tweed. The colour stays when you break and replace it.

Behind the scenes you sit on an invisible seat entity (encodedlogistics:seat) that the chair makes for you. It goes away as soon as you get up or the chair is gone, and it can't be summoned on its own.
