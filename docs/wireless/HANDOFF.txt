ENCODED LOGISTICS - HANDOFF: WIRELESS (Access Point, Wireless Bridge, Wireless Ingress / Egress Ports, Wireless Controller update)
====================================================================================================================================
REPLACES the earlier wireless package (never implemented). Scope as decided: ONE Access Point; the existing rack Wireless
Controller keeps its infinite range and Handheld Terminal linking; a Wireless Bridge block with infinite range, linked to the
controller, that brings whatever is cabled to it onto the controller's network; wireless versions of the Ingress and Egress
Ports only (QIO-style). The other wireless devices, the Wireless Remote and the Handheld Fabrication Terminal are dropped.

HOW WIRELESS WORKS
------------------
- Wireless Controller (rack device, rack/device/WirelessControllerDevice.java): the brain. Infinite range in its dimension
  (wirelessCrossDimension as today for other dimensions). Links Handheld Terminals exactly as now (use the terminal on its
  rack). New: links Wireless Bridges and Wireless Ports with a Link Card (use the card on the controller's unit, then on the
  block). Its wireless clients (bridges and ports) work only while at least one Access Point is online on its network;
  with none it faults "No access points" and its bridges and ports go offline. Handheld Terminals keep working as today
  (no AP needed - see open question 1).
- Access Point (block): the radio. Cabled to the network like any device (1 lane uplink). Each online AP adds 8 client
  slots for that network's controller; bridges and ports beyond the total disconnect (most recently linked first) until
  slots free up.
- Wireless Bridge (block): a remote network segment. Linked to a controller, anything cabled to the bridge (devices,
  storage, terminals) is part of the controller's network, anywhere in range (infinite). Carries up to 32 lanes [config];
  counts as 1 client.
- Wireless Ingress / Egress Port (block): mounted on an inventory face (like Mekanism's QIO Importer / Exporter): pulls
  from / pushes into that inventory for the controller's network, no cable. Same filters, modules and redstone modes as
  the cabled ports (part/PortPart.java logic, the port GUI), with a link header. 1 lane each, 1 client each.
- Energy: bridges and ports draw over the air at wirelessEnergyMultiplier [config 2.0] x their cabled equivalent's
  drain; APs [2 FE/t].
Reviewed against repo HEAD (pulled this session). Paths relative to the repo root; the zip mirrors them under src/.

3. LINKING, CAPACITY, LANES
=====================================================================================================
  - Link: use a Link Card on the Wireless Controller's rack unit (stores the controller), then on a bridge or port (it
    stores the controller id; state linking -> online once an AP is up). Unlink from the controller's Linked tab, or by
    applying a card from another controller. Requires the configure permission (Firewall) on the controller's network.
  - Capacity: clients = linked bridges + ports; slots = 8 x online APs [config wirelessApClients 8]; over capacity the most
    recently linked disconnect (state fault, HUD "Over capacity") and reconnect automatically.
  - Lanes: port 1, bridge up to 32 (what its cabled side uses) - remote links to the controller's rack node.
  - No AP online: controller fault NO_ACCESS_POINTS; bridges and ports offline (fault).
